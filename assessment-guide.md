# Assessment guide: Pick for me's judged questions

The standard every assessment pass follows, so a pass next year judges books
the same way as this one. **Change this file first** whenever a question or
its answers change, then reassess only what the change affects.

Answer choices settled by the owner on 2026-11-18. Definitions are a first
draft; the owner's example books (below) and the 50-book trial will sharpen them.

## Rules for every question

- One answer per book (per work: every edition shares it) per question.
- Each answer is marked **sure** or **unsure**. Unsure means: I don't know the
  book well and the description and subject headings didn't settle it.
- **The owner's own answers are final.** A pass never overwrites an answer the
  owner set.
- **Doesn't apply** is an answer too (for example, pace for a reference book).
  In Pick for me, it lets the book through, like a missing answer.
- Sources, in order: what I know of the book; its saved publisher description,
  LibraryThing genres and LC subject headings; Wikidata for authors; a web
  search of reviews only for books still unsure.

## 6. International

**International** or **Domestic**.

- International: the book is **translated**, or the author **grew up outside
  the US**. Either one is enough.
- Domestic: written in English by an author who grew up in the US.
- Where the author grew up, not citizenship or birthplace: Jhumpa Lahiri
  (born in London, raised in the US) is domestic; Nabokov (grew up in Russia)
  is international. Wikidata gives birthplace and citizenship, which usually
  settle it; immigrant authors are judged and marked unsure.
- Several authors (anthologies): international if most of the book is.
- Judged per author where possible (one answer covers all their books), but a
  translated book is international whoever wrote it.

## 7. Character- or plot-driven

Stories (fiction, comics, drama, myth, and story-like poetry):
**Character** / **Both** / **Plot**.

- Character: the pull is who the people are and how they change (a quiet
  literary novel about a marriage).
- Plot: the pull is what happens next (a thriller, an adventure).
- Both: neither clearly leads.

Nonfiction (including biography, memoir and essays): **Narrative** / **Both** / **Ideas**.

- Narrative: reads like a story, with people and events in order (a memoir,
  narrative history).
- Ideas: an argument, a survey, or a reference work.
- Both: neither clearly leads.

Most poetry collections: doesn't apply.

## 8. Pace

**Quick** / **Steady** / **Slow**.

- Quick: pulls you along; short chapters, momentum, easy prose.
- Slow: rewards patience; dense prose, long reflection, little happening.
- Steady: everything between.
- Reference books and similar: doesn't apply.

## 9. Mood

**Light** / **In between** / **Heavy**.

- Light: comforting, funny or easygoing, even if it has sad moments.
- Heavy: grief, violence, cruelty or despair at its center, or emotionally
  demanding throughout, however well written.
- In between: real weight, but not oppressive (most literary fiction lands here).

## Owner's example books

20–30 books the owner has read, rated on each question, in
`data/assessment-examples.md`. They name the owner's books, so they stay out
of this public repo (`data/` is git-ignored). **They outweigh the definitions
above when the two disagree;** read them before every pass. Once the
assessments table exists, they're also saved there as the owner's answers,
so they're in the database backups too.
